Can we Talk?

Patrick C. Cox 24 June 2025 5 min read

There's no shortage of opinions, hypotheses or hype regarding the impact artificial intelligence will have on the way we live and work. That's the reality: industrial revolutions produce step-change productivity improvements, but not without socio-economic displacement and pain in the short term.

During the first revolution, the entire cottage shoe-cobbler industry became obsolete. In the third, droves of horses found themselves out of work — along with the stables. To find work, it was often necessary for the displaced to migrate to cities for factory jobs. During these periods of re-balancing, governments, churches and community groups had to provide displaced workers economic support for survival. As we move collectively into the fourth industrial revolution, very few certainties exist — except change.

There is a common thread through the previous three industrial revolutions. The objective in each case was to use technology — the steam engine, say — to multiply human and animal force, power and energy, making tasks easier, faster and cheaper. The fourth event is quite different: the force being magnified is human cognitive ability.

The internet is the wrong benchmark

Prior to generative artificial intelligence, the most impactful innovation on society was the internet. It changed the way we work, shop, learn, date, manage time and book travel. For researchers it was transformative in both the ease and the quality of access to information. In some cases it democratised access to information and the ability to learn new skills. Could you imagine life without it?

By comparison, the forecast impact of generative AI promises to be greater by an order of magnitude. Even in its infancy, prognosticators forecast massive societal impacts: the democratisation of quality health care, education and transportation, and potentially the reduction of greenhouse gases. On economics, Goldman Sachs has predicted the technology will drive global GDP up 7% while improving productivity by 1.5%. Tech giants, private equity, venture capital and thousands of start-ups are betting their futures on it — some $225B invested from 2015 to 2022.

Every job will be affected. Some jobs will be created, but every job will be affected. Jensen Huang, CEO, NVIDIA

The technology is showing tremendous promise

  • Autonomous taxis have moved past the historical adoption hurdle of 5%, pointing to mass adoption in San Francisco and ramping up in Austin.
  • AI-driven apps such as Canva, Deep Dream Generator and Runway are democratising creative development, and point to a challenging path forward for many advertising agencies.
  • AI now excels at detecting disease, such as cancer from MRIs and X-rays. (Data Science UA, Top AI Achievements 2025)
  • Adoption among companies has surged to 72–78%, against 55% a year earlier, according to McKinsey's 2024 global survey. ChatGPT reached one million users in two days — adoption velocity not seen previously with any innovation.

Let's dig into that 1.5% productivity gain

While job cuts are not the only contributor to productivity as measured, they are the largest, and the most significant in terms of potential societal impact. In much of the current AI press, job eliminations are mentioned as an afterthought — somewhat like the legal disclosures at the end of a pharmaceutical advertisement, where the announcer whispers and spits the words so quickly that Eminem would be impressed.

AI's cognitive capabilities create productivity by mimicking and amplifying human cognitive abilities. As of writing, AI is producing parity capability with humans in several measured cognitive areas, and improving in most, if not all, rapidly. With theoretically unlimited learning capacity, it is easy to deduce that it will not take long for humans to no longer be the smartest in the room.

There is a second difference between AI and previous technology advancements: the speed and scale of deployment and adoption. ChatGPT registered its millionth user in just two days. That velocity gives credence to the aggressive timing in the forecasts.

Despite its impressive learning capacity, AI can't do everything by itself. While it is capable of delivering answers to complex and challenging questions, it needs someone to ask the right question. And it lacks emotion, making it ill-suited to a wide variety of professions where human connection is necessary or beneficial.

AI could wipe out half of all entry-level white-collar jobs — and spike unemployment to 10–20% in the next one to five years. Dario Amodei, CEO, Anthropic

What industry experts predict

  • 300 million full-time jobs could be replaced globally by generative AI technologies, according to Goldman Sachs. (Financial Times, "IMF Warns of Major Disruption to Labor", Darren Dodd, 17 June 2024)
  • McKinsey predicts that by 2030, up to 375 million workers globally — about 14% of the workforce — may need to switch occupational categories due to AI-driven automation. (McKinsey, 2024 State of AI)
  • Up to 73 million US jobs could be at risk of automation by 2030, according to long-term projections.

Status

AI is already breaking "the bottom rungs of the career ladder", with junior software developers, paralegals and retail workers at high risk, according to Aneesh Raman, Chief Economic Opportunity Officer at LinkedIn. (Technology Magazine, 9 June 2025)

  • 1.9 million US jobs have already been affected by AI as of early 2025, with significant impacts in IT services, finance and customer support.
  • Microsoft laid off 6,000 in May, mainly software engineers, its CEO having noted that AI now writes nearly 30% of their code.
  • Chatbots and generative AI tools have replaced more human roles in two years than robotic process automation did in the past decade.
  • AI has reduced entry-level legal assistant jobs by 33% in the US since 2023, mainly through document automation and e-discovery tools.
  • In India, AI has displaced over 650,000 IT service jobs, primarily in Tier-1 outsourcing cities such as Bengaluru and Hyderabad.
  • Klarna has laid off 40% of its workforce and attributed the reduction to AI efficiency. (The Economic Times, 18 May 2025)
  • Germany reported a 17% decline in manufacturing jobs attributed to AI adoption in factories between 2023 and 2024. (The Economic Times, 18 May 2025)
  • Japan has implemented robotics at large scale, resulting in the loss of 490,000 warehouse and assembly-line jobs by early 2025.
  • In Canada, AI adoption in insurance and banking led to a 12% reduction in back-office roles in 2024.

So what can we do in preparation?

  1. Stay close to the front end of AI engagement. The internet created millions of jobs. There was a point when nobody knew what a social network marketing manager did — and then they became indispensable. AI will spawn new businesses and verticals as it matures. One of the first and most obvious is prompt engineering.
  2. Invest in developing your soft skills — AI lacks these: emotional intelligence, creative problem solving, empathy, negotiation and persuasion, and leadership.
  3. Learn AI. Become part of the evolution.
  4. Focus on results. AI handles tasks.

This is clearly a transition point for our society. We will continue to provide insights and updates. In the meantime, best of luck to each of you on your personal journey.
